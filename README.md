# Distant Worlds

Press **New world**: a new planet is born, with its own landscape, creatures and generative soundtrack.
Everything comes from a seed in the URL (`…/#v1-8393_2007_4`): share the link and whoever opens it sees and hears the same world, at the same spot (`#seed@12.5`).

- **Your explorer**: a character with looks random for each player, which you can change in the Atlas (**Your explorer**: suit colour, skin, headgear, backpack; kept in this browser), walks left and right (arrow keys / A-D, hold ◀ ▶, or drag), or on its own with **🚶 Auto**: it keeps going the way it faces and gathers whatever comes within reach. Auto burns fuel (1 per screen) and stops when the tank is empty; walking by hand burns nothing and earns fuel on new ground. Auto goes on in a background tab (a worker's timer drives it), and so does the colony time miner, only while auto-walking (Freenet and web alike).
- **First steps** (🎓): a new explorer gets a five-step tour (walk, gather, jump, Atlas, Auto), each step completed by doing it; skippable, and run again from the ☰ menu. Existing players (three worlds or more) do not get it. The Atlas also keeps **your numbers**, only in the browser: play time, what was gathered, fuel earned, spent and lost to a full tank, builds, jumps and battles, per minute, with a button to copy them (used to tune the economy: fuel cells are now worth 10 and 40% at most of the ground, since fuel used to refill far faster than it was spent).
- **Rooms** (🚪): about one building in four on the nearest ground can be walked into (`world.js` `foreStructsNear`, own stream, the world's own kit, never at the arrival spot): its front fades as the explorer steps in and a lit room shows, with a chest that holds fuel, plants or ore, the same for everyone each day (`atlas.js` `chestLoot`). Not on worlds walked into. Pure client logic, the same on Freenet and web.
- **Jumping** (⤴): low obstacles (a rock, a log, a crate, ice; `world.js` `obstaclesNear`, own random stream, none at the arrival spot) stop the explorer at their edge unless it is higher than they are. Space, ↑ / W or the ⤴ button jump (on a world walked into ↑ walks and only Space jumps); a jump is lower and shorter on a heavy world; a leap also carries the explorer faster. Auto-walk jumps by itself (`atlas.js` `shouldLeap`, `walkBy`, `jumpStep`; tested to clear every obstacle at gravity ×1, ×1.5 and ×2). Pure client logic, the same on Freenet and web.
- **Paths** (🛤): about one world in six is walked into rather than across (`world.js` `view: 'depth'`, its own random stream so every `v1` world keeps everything else). The two walkable layers lie flat towards the horizon: a path narrows into the distance, what lies ahead stands to either side and grows as you approach, the far ranges stay put. ↑ ↓ (or ◀ ▶, or dragging) walk along it; things are taken when they reach your feet. You see about four screens ahead, everything is drawn from far to near, the explorer is seen from behind, and a haze of the sky's colour thickens towards the horizon.
- **Endless worlds**: walk forever. Terrain, structures, flora, creatures and rare relics are generated chunk by chunk as you go.
- **Cultures** (One Piece style: real places, reinvented): temperate, japanese, nordic, desert, jungle, western, arctic, apocalyptic, war. Each brings its buildings (pagodas and torii, longhouses and stave churches, domes and minarets, step pyramids, saloons, igloos and lighthouses, broken towers and wrecks, bunkers and searchlights), flora (blossoms, bamboo, palms, cacti, dead trees…), relief, colours, weather (petals, snow, ash) and music.
- **Relief and depth**: rolling hills, ridged peaks, mesas, dunes, rock spires, floating islands with waterfalls; rivers winding towards the viewer; auroras and smoke columns; seas that lie between the far ranges and the walkable shore; volcanoes (some erupting) and waterfalls pouring down the ranges. Volcanoes and waterfalls come from their own random stream, so every `v1` world keeps everything it had.
- **Traits**: era (ancient … far future), civilization (uninhabited, humans, aliens, ruins), fauna (birds, herds, sky jellyfish, giants, flying whales), flora (forest, conifers, crystals, mushrooms) and visual style (classic, neon, pastel, mono, sepia).
- **Living galaxy**: worlds are born, age and collapse on a clock shared by everyone. Each star lives 30-120 days; when its world collapses it gives birth to a new one (`#v1-x_y_n.g`, the g-th world of that star; old links lead to the new world). From age 70 a world is unstable: tremors, a red sky, meteors, richer ore, and gravity rising up to ×2 (slower steps, shorter reach, buildings cost more). If it collapses while you are there you jump away in an emergency and leave half your bag behind. A dying home can be moved to a young world (🚀, 3 plants + 3 ore per building); otherwise its buildings are lost and its creatures escape with you.
- **Heritage**: a world born from a star remembers the one before it. Ruins of its buildings stand here and there, one of its species survives (and can be captured), and the homes explorers built there are left as ruins: yours (remembered when your home collapsed) and, on Freenet, everyone else's, with their names.
- **Events**, the same for every explorer at the same time: every 3 hours a world may get one, lasting 1-2 hours. A meteor shower leaves meteorites with 4-7 ore (more showers on dying worlds), a herd migrates in from a star of the same sector and can be captured, a trader swaps plants and ore for ore, fuel or a creature from another world. The map marks worlds with an event now.
- **Expeditions** (🧭, Atlas): every 14 days a new season, the same for every explorer: five stages to reach in order, each a kind of world to find (one trait for the first two, two for the rest). Each stage gives ⚡50, the last one the season's badge (achievement Pathfinder). Pure function of the date (`atlas.js` `expedition`), so the Freenet and web versions agree with no server.
- **Battles** (🗡, ☰ menu, off by default): wild creatures of the world, drawn in red, wait on some stretches, the same for everyone each day (`world.js` `foesNear`). Reach one and you fight it, with up to 3 creatures from your home at your side (the strongest that walk, `battleParty`): each round everyone strikes once, the fastest first, and the creature hits one of you at random. Its parts give its stats (size: life and strength, horns: harder blows, legs and pace: speed; `atlas.js` `foeStats`), and so they do for your creatures. Wild creatures are tougher than the same species at your side (×1.6 life, ×1.8 blows) and have a level: one more every 4 screens from the arrival point, one more on dying worlds, one more for every two of your battle levels, and ⭐ elite ones (one in five) are two above that. Every third round a wild creature gathers its strength and then hits twice as hard: guard (🛡, no blow, half the damage to your side) that round. Your creatures bring 70% of their life and 30% of their blows to a battle. Each creature fights with the element of its home world's culture (a bred one, of its first wild ancestor; `atlas.js` `elementOf`): 🔥 desert/war/apocalyptic burns 🌿 jungle/temperate, which holds against 🌀 japanese/western, which blows away ❄ nordic/arctic, which puts out 🔥; a blow on the element it beats hits ×1.5, on the one that beats it ×0.75 (you have no element). Creatures grow with the battles they fight: each win gives every creature at your side an xp, and its level (1 + √xp) adds life and blows. The party is picked for the foe: the creatures with the best strength × element edge against its element (`battleParty`). Once a battle your ✨ Special move hits twice; 🌿 Heal eats 3 plants instead of your blow (+15 life); worn down to a quarter, a creature can be 🐾 captured (6 times in 10, otherwise it strikes back). Win and you take ore and count a win: your battle level (1 + √wins) adds life and attack, and 25 wins unlock Champion. Lose and you escape with half your bag, or flee for ⚡5. A blow shows: the foe shakes when it is hit, the dialog flashes red when one of you is (no shaking with reduced motion). Pure client logic, the same on Freenet and web.
- **Ilen's diary** (📜, Atlas): an explorer who left her dying star wrote 100 pages and scattered them across the worlds. About one world in three keeps one, at a fixed spot near the arrival point (`world.js` `pageNear`); each page you take is the next one of the story (`diary.js`), so it reads in order whichever worlds you find them on. Finding all 100 unlocks Reader. Kept with the rest of your progress, the same on Freenet and web.
- **Chronicle** (Atlas): the history of the star you are on, newest world first (up to 8): its name, rarity, when it was born and collapsed, who lived there, and which species survived into the next world (`world.js` `chronicle`).
- **Languages** (🗣): each culture, and the aliens, speaks its own language, the same on every world (`world.js` `lexicon`). Every new inhabited star teaches one word of it, every new relic two, in a fixed order; buildings and relics carry inscriptions (point at them) where the words you know are translated. The Atlas lists your vocabulary; learning all 24 words of one language unlocks Polyglot. Kept with the rest of your progress.
- **Galaxy**: every world is a star on a map (10,000 × 10,000 sectors, `#v1-<x>_<y>_<n>`). Regions such as the Alien Nebula or the Sky Seas change what grows there.
- **Composable species**: animals are built from parts (legs, neck, head, horns, tail, pattern) and go into your bestiary. Each species has its own size, from tiny to huge (×0.55 to ×2.6, giants even bigger), and so does each plant species, up to giant trees (×3.5). The Atlas shows each one's size, and the extremes are rarer finds (a huge or tiny species counts towards rare, epic, legendary; a giant plant is at least rare). On worlds of giants, titans sometimes pass: beasts so huge only their legs fit on screen (too big to capture). Flyers (bird flocks, sky jellyfish, flying whales) are species too: capture one when it passes over you; at home it circles above your buildings (it does not breed).
- **Herbarium**: each world grows 1-3 plant species of its flora (own proportions, colour, sometimes flowers, a Latin-sounding name); the ones you see are pressed into the Atlas.
- **Pickups** along the walk, the same for everyone each day and more frequent far from the arrival point: fuel cells (+10 fuel, shaped by the culture), map fragments (the next jump costs half) and, on the Freenet version, time shards (your time on that planet runs ×3 for 90 s; 3 a day per planet). Click one near the middle of the screen, where you stand.
- **Gathering**: near your explorer, click (or press **E**) to harvest plants 🌿, capture creatures 🐾 and mine ore deposits ⛏. What you take is gone for the day and grows back tomorrow.
- **Home planet** (🏠): settle on a world, then come back for free with **Go home**. Creatures you captured are released there and roam around, and the seeds of every plant species you harvested (the first harvest gives them) are sown in a garden among your buildings; plants and ore build huts, mills, stables, workshops, temples, castles, towers and domes where you stand (**🏗 Build**), growing your camp into a village, town, city, capital. **Population**: huts, castles and domes house people, mills, stables and domes feed them (the land alone feeds 6); each inhabitant adds 2% to what the home produces (at most double), walks among the buildings (all alike, also on homes other explorers see), and eats 0.1 plants an hour from the bag; when the bag runs out the hungry starve, and the empty homes fill again (one person every 4 hours fed). Starvation is kept in the browser: published homes (Freenet and web alike) show the people their buildings can hold. **Civilization level** comes from people, buildings (their cost and variety) and creatures, and gives the title. **Pirates** may attack a home every 8 hours (a shared clock, the same raids for everyone; new homes are spared for 3 days): crews grow with the civilization level, castles and towers (plus one per 5 people and one per 3 creatures) defend. Repelled pirates leave ore; winners damage a building, which does nothing until repaired for half its cost, and the home is then spared for a day (a lost raid shakes the screen red, a won one plays a fanfare). Buildings work for you: they produce plants, ore or fuel while you are away (up to a day's worth, collected when you come home), mills / workshops / stables add to what you gather, towers make every jump cheaper (10% each, up to 50%). Achievements: Settler, Founder, Mayor, Architect, Rancher. Kept in this browser (and in the node on Freenet), visible only to you.
- **What your home needs** (🏗 Build): five bars for what the home lacks, from `atlas.js` `homeNeeds`: food stock (the plants in the bag, against a day of food for the people), food (what the land feeds against the places for people), homes (the other way round), defence against the biggest raid (castles, towers, one per 5 people, one per 3 creatures), and stables (one per 8 creatures). The weakest bar is named with the cheapest building that mends it (`homeAdvice`), marked ⭐ in the list. Pure client logic, the same on Freenet and web.
- **Fleet** (🚀): each Stable or Workshop at home gives a ship (at most 3). From 🏗 Build a ship flies to a star you have visited (fuel as a jump from home, 20-100; 1-4 hours by distance), gathers plants and ore (about 3-7 and 1-4 an hour, sometimes a plant species for the herbarium and its seeds), and comes back even while the game is closed; the loot is collected at home. Dying worlds give up to double, but the ship may be lost (half the world's instability; always on a world that collapsed). The same voyage always gives the same (`atlas.js` `shipLoot`). Kept in the browser like the home, so Freenet and the web version behave the same.
- **Breeding** (🧬): each Stable at home lets two species living there have a young one a day (🌿10 ⛏5). The young takes each part from one parent, sometimes a new one (a mutation), with proportions in between, and a name from both; the same parents always give the same young (`world.js` `breed`). Its id joins the parents' ids, sorted, a bred parent in parentheses (`a+b`, `(a+b)+c`), so it can be checked and redrawn anywhere; young breed in turn, up to 3 generations from the wild. It joins the bestiary and the home, and on Freenet / the web version other explorers see it there.
- **Names** (✎, Freenet / web): the first explorer to name a species (wild or bred) or a plant (wild or crossed) from the Atlas gives it its common name, signed and kept by the galaxy (earliest wins); everyone sees it on hover cards and in their Atlas.
- **Messages** stay longer the longer they are, and the **📜 Log** (☰ menu) keeps this session's last 100. On a phone the world's details take one line: tap the name to read them all.
- **Hover cards**: point at (or tap) a plant, animal, building, relic, flyer or flag to see what it is, and whether it is already in your atlas or bestiary.
- **Music** (`audio.js`): each world has its own lead instrument (bell, glass, pluck, marimba, flute), drone (organ, wind, choir), optional pad / bass / percussion and reverb; it gets richer when more is happening on screen. A battle or a world close to collapse crossfades into tense music and back; songs fade on jump and mute; the lead swells with each phrase, and a world's `density` sets how often the melody rests.
- **Composer** (`compose.js`): real harmony instead of a loop. Each world plays in one of three styles (ballad, dreamy, groove), and a world close to collapse switches to a fourth, tense, with chord progressions, phrased melodies (question and answer, cadences on the tonic, A A B A form), accompaniment (broken chords, arpeggios or stabs) and bass, all inside the world's own scale. What it knows comes partly from **example MIDI files**: `node tools/learn-music.mjs *.mid > music-model.js` learns chord-to-chord moves, melodic intervals, rhythm cells and tempo per style (statistics only, no notes). The model shipped here was learned from 178 MIDI transcriptions (not in the repo): the Final Fantasy VIII and IX soundtracks and five Metal Gear Solid tracks. Minor, fast pieces teach the tense style, slow ones the ballad, steady beats the groove, the rest the dreamy one. The learner also reads RMID files (a MIDI inside a RIFF wrapper). Add more examples and regenerate the model to teach it more. Variety: each section has three rhythm cells and two fill every bar (the rhythm turns over every four bars), the answer phrase moves against the question's contour, and the lead waits two bars at the start of every pass. Each pass (32 bars) is dressed differently: the accompaniment is a piano, a harp or a kalimba (pluck, marimba or kalimba in the driving styles), the bass a triangle or a buzzier saw, the pad comes and goes, and in the B section the lead gives way to a second voice (bell ↔ glass, pluck → marimba → kalimba, flute → strings) with a slow bowed counter-line above.
- **Sound** (`audio.js`): voices sit in their own place across the stereo field (bass in the middle), the echo is a ping-pong delay whose repeats thin out each time round, plucked instruments are Karplus-Strong strings, and the output goes through a limiter and a soft clipper so stacked voices never distort. The techniques come from two sibling projects, [eph](https://github.com/scobru/eph) and [cabasa](https://github.com/scobru/cabasa). Audio is shared, so the Freenet and web versions sound the same. Each world also has the sounds of its place under the music, from its traits: waves on a sea, a brook by a river, crickets at night, birds by day where birds live, a rumble near volcanoes (`world.js` `music.ambience`). Battles, diary pages and healing have their own sound effects.
- **Updates** (☰ 📰): what changed in the game, in players' words, newest first. The same on Freenet and web.
- **Game**, all in the browser (`localStorage`, no server):
  - **rarity**: weighted traits make each world common / rare / epic / legendary;
  - **atlas**: discovered traits, civilization × era combinations, relics, achievements, bestiary, logbook of saved worlds;
  - **world of the day** (a star picked from the date) with a daily mission and streak;
  - **hyperdrive fuel**: jumping to an unvisited world costs fuel (nearby stars 20–50, a random jump 100). You refuel by walking new ground and discovering things; the daily mission fills the tank. Revisiting stars, saved worlds, the world of the day and shared links are free.
- Seeds carry the generator version (`v1-…`): a future v2 must keep generating `v1` seeds the same way.
- Static, no dependencies, no build: `index.html` + ES modules. Deterministic: `makeWorld(seed)` is a pure function (`node world.test.mjs && node atlas.test.mjs`).

Local: `python3 -m http.server` and open http://localhost:8000.

## Freenet version

The same app can be published on [Freenet](https://freenet.org). There it becomes multiplayer:

- **Sign in** with your [Viso](https://github.com/scobru/viso) persona (it delegates a key kept by your node, as in FreeNames; your **name and avatar come from your Viso profile**), or **create / import a key** right in the game, as in the other apps. Every colony and message is signed for that persona. A key made here can be backed up from the Atlas. Your progress (atlas, fuel, logbook) is also kept in the node, since the page runs in a sandbox that may have no `localStorage`.
- **Persona level**: colonizing needs a level (`ante`) of at least **20 bits**, chatting at least **16**. The level is the persona's own proof of work, made once: in Viso, or here for a key made in the game (about 2^20 hashes, some seconds; byte for byte the proof viso-ante makes). Colonies and messages carry it and the contracts verify it, so throwaway personas cost something.
- **Colonies**: one colonist per planet. Stay on a planet and your browser measures the time as proof of work, mined in the background at a fixed honest rate (5000 hashes/s). After a few minutes a **🚩 Plant flag** button appears: your flag goes where you stand, and everyone sees it in the world and on the map (gold ring). Planets keep their generated names. **Spend more time there than its colonist** and the button becomes **Take this planet**; they can come back and take it again (a colonist's extra time is published quietly, so staying defends the planet). **More colonies need more work, checked by the contract**: a persona's colonies, weakest first, must show the minimum work for the first, then 1.5× more for each next (`colony_limit` in `contract/`, `colonyLimit` in `freenet/colony.js`, the same in `web/db.js`). A second colony needs about 1.5× the time of the first, a sixth about 7.6×, and one that does not meet it is dropped from the state, whatever client sent it. The time shown on the button already includes it. A flag also **costs resources** from your bag: ⛏100 ore and 🌿150 plants for the first colony, 1.8× more with each colony you already hold, double when you take a planet from someone (`atlas.js` `colonyCost`). The cost is shown on the button. Resources are private to your browser, so this is a rule of the client (the same on Freenet and web), not something the contracts can check.
- **Homes**: once signed in (level ≥ 20), your home planet is published to the galaxy contract: whoever lands there sees your buildings and creatures, with your name and civilization title. The newest signed home of each persona wins; a home lists at most 64 building parts and 64 creatures (the newest), and declares its whole size (`size`: parts, creatures, people, civilization points, signed as `|size:…`), which the contract bounds: at least what is listed, at most 10 people a part, no more points than that many parts and creatures could give, at most 10 000 parts and 100 000 creatures. The 🏗 Build dialog says when others see only part of your home.
- **Sieges** (⚔): land on another explorer's home and press **⚔ Besiege** (⚡20 ⛏10). Stay there: a second miner, bound to that home and the day (`sha256("dwr1|target|attacker|day|lane|nonce")`), measures the siege; every 2 minutes of it is one raider. **Attack** publishes the raid, signed, to the galaxy contract (at least the work of a colony, one raid per attacker, home and day, a longer siege replaces a shorter one). Raiders against the home's defence (castles, towers, people, creatures, as published) decide it: the attacker takes ore, or the defence holds. The defender's browser fights each new raid like a pirate raid (a building damaged and a day of shield, or ore; the screen shakes red on a loss, and the attacker hears a fanfare or a fall), new homes are spared for 3 days. Homes marked **🕊 Peaceful** neither besiege nor get besieged, except for a day after their owner besieged someone.
- **Leaderboard** (🏆): explorers ranked by civilization level (from their published home's declared size: people, buildings, creatures; older homes without it count what they list), then colonies held and time invested, read straight from the contract; your rank even outside the top 20, and each explorer's planets, one click away.
- **Galaxy chat**: a hideable side panel (💬 Chat, with an unread count). It is live, not an archive: only the last 15 minutes are shown, and the contract keeps just a small relay buffer (newest 3 per persona, 50 overall), so nothing piles up on Freenet. Messages are signed for your persona; the Viso level is the anti-spam, so sending is instant. **🌍 Share world** posts the world you are on as a card (name, rarity, era, colonist) that takes whoever clicks it to the same spot, for free. Whoever shares a position here (the card carries `@x`) shows up for the next 15 minutes as a faint **ghost** on that planet, at that spot, with their name and how long ago they were there; it fades with time, and is drawn on side-scrolling worlds (not the ones walked into). It is the same chat data on both versions: no new record, nothing to prove. **Shared links now land where they were shared** (the card carried a layer coordinate before, read as the explorer's position).
- **Explorers who are here now** (👁): while you play, your browser says where you stand (a `Here` record in the chat contract, `dwp1|persona|nick|ts|<seed>@<position>`, signed like a message, persona level ≥ 16 bits): when you arrive on a planet, at most every 20 s while you move, every minute while you stay. Explorers on the same planet are drawn where they last said they were, with their name, walking there at a run (jumping if far); after 2 minutes without news they disappear. One record per persona (the newer replaces it), the newest 100 kept. **☰ 👁 Visible to others** turns it off: you then fade out of the others' worlds. It is near-live, not real time (a step every 20 s), the same on Freenet and web: Freenet has no direct channel between browsers, so a smooth real-time version would only be possible on the web and is left out to keep both alike. Not on worlds walked into.

How time is measured: for each of 8 lanes the miner keeps the lowest `sha256("dwc1|seed|persona|lane|nonce")`. The sum of their top 64 bits shrinks as work accumulates, so it estimates the hashes spent (about 64·2⁶⁴ / sum). Lanes are bound to the planet and the persona, so time cannot be moved elsewhere. The contract keeps the lowest sum per planet; equal sums fall back to the colonist's newer update, so every merge order converges.

| Part | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `contract/` | Rust galaxy contract: `seed → colony {persona, name, nick, x, ts, nonces, ante, sig, cert}`, plus homes, sieges (`raids`) and common names (`names`, species or plant id → first name). Checks the Viso delegation (`whoiam-delegation`, from FreeNames), the signature, the minimum work and the persona level (`viso-ante`, ≥ 20 bits). Admin-signed blocklist (`dwb2\|galaxy\|ts\|list`) hides stars or personas. Parameters = admin ed25519 key (`ADMIN` in `freenet/net.js`, the FreeNames / FreeTunes admin key) ‖ app path. |
| `chat/` | Rust chat contract: messages signed like colonies, persona level ≥ 16 bits, a small live buffer (newest 3 per persona, 50 overall; clients show the last 15 minutes), and one presence per persona (where it stands now, newest 100; clients show the last 2 minutes); the admin blocklist removes personas. |
| `freenet/chat.js` | Chat rules in JS. |
| `freenet/viso.js` | Reads a Viso persona (contract address, signed slots, profile, avatar, `ante` level) and builds a level for a key held here. Checked against vectors made with viso-core / viso-ante (`freenet/viso.vector.json`). |
| `freenet/colony.js` | Colony rules in JS and the time miner. Shares a test vector with the contract. |
| `freenet/connect.js` | Viso sign-in proof and key display, checked against whoiam's golden vector. |
| `freenet/net.js` | Node connection (`@freenetorg/freenet-stdlib`), identity delegate (FreeNames' `identity.wasm`), sign-in, galaxy contract. |
| `freenet/ui.js` | Game layer: miner loop, auto-colonize / take-over, status line, atlas sections. Only imported when a node serves the page or with `?node=host:port`, so the static site stays build-free. |

Known limits:

- **The time rate is honest, not enforced.** Freenet has no trusted clock: a modified client can mine faster and take planets sooner. The Viso level only makes each persona cost something; it does not slow down a fast miner.
- A level shows a persona cost something, not that it is unique or human: someone can still grind many personas.
- Profiles and avatars are read from the persona's Viso contract when this node can reach it; a key made in the game has no avatar. Its secret is kept in the node's store: back it up, since signing out forgets it.
- One node = one explorer: the app key lives in the node, so every browser on the same node shares the sign-in (use Sign out to switch persona).
- Names come from Viso profiles (or are chosen here when the profile has none): signed, but not unique.
- Names go to the earliest self-declared time: a modified client can backdate a name, as with chat times.
- Sieges are proven work, but who wins is decided by each browser against a self-declared home, and the damage is applied by the defender's own browser: a modified client can ignore it (as it can already inflate its home).
- Homes are self-declared: resources are gathered in the browser, so a modified client can publish a bigger home than it earned. The contract only bounds it (64 buildings, 64 creatures, signed, persona level ≥ 20).
- The admin can hide stars or personas; changing the admin key, the app path or the contract creates a new, empty galaxy.
- Colonies are public and permanent on the network (no global delete).
- Chat times are the authors' own (no trusted clock): clients hide messages from the future, and each persona keeps at most 3 messages, which bounds what fake times can do.
- On a node that does not have the contracts yet, the first visitor waits about 5 s while they are created.

### Build and publish

Requirements: `rustup` with the `wasm32-unknown-unknown` target, `freenet` and `fdev`, Node 20+.

```bash
cd contract && cargo test --release && cargo build --release --target wasm32-unknown-unknown && cd ..
cd chat && cargo test --release && cargo build --release --target wasm32-unknown-unknown && cd ..
cp contract/target/wasm32-unknown-unknown/release/distant_worlds_galaxy.wasm freenet/galaxy.wasm
cp chat/target/wasm32-unknown-unknown/release/distant_worlds_chat.wasm freenet/chat.wasm
npm install && npm test && npm run build          # -> dist/

# dev against a local node (no IPv6? add --ws-api-address 127.0.0.1)
freenet local --ws-api-port 7510 --config-dir .devnode/config --data-dir .devnode/data --log-dir .devnode/log
npm run dev   # open http://localhost:5173/?node=127.0.0.1:7510  (add &rate=150000 to colonize in seconds)

fdev website init distant-worlds                  # once: creates the signing key. Back it up!
```

## Web version (SQLite)

The same multiplayer game without Freenet: a small Node server keeps the galaxy (colonies, homes, sieges, blocklist) and the chat (the same small live buffer) in a SQLite file, using Node's built-in `node:sqlite` (Node 22.13+, no new dependency).

- Same records, same rules: `web/db.js` ports the contracts' checks (signatures, the whoiam delegation, the persona level, the proof of work, limits) and merge rules, so the client code (`freenet/ui.js`) is unchanged. `web/net.js` replaces `freenet/net.js` in the web build (`vite --mode web`).
- No Viso: explorers create or import a key in the game (kept in `localStorage`, backed up from the Atlas). Its level is built in the browser as on Freenet.
- Same checks on both sides of the wire: the checks live in `freenet/check.js` (pure, no node or browser imports), which `web/db.js` runs on every record it takes and the client (`freenet/ui.js`) runs again before it shows or acts on any record another explorer published: a lying server or node cannot make you see a flag, home, name or message that was not signed, or damage your home with a forged siege. This works on Freenet too. See `SECURITY.md`.
- Web-only defences (not part of the contracts): security headers (a CSP, no framing, no sniffing), a rate limit per client (10 posts in a burst then 30 a minute; 30 reads then 120 a minute), at most 20 event streams per client and 2000 in all, request bodies up to 64 KB, at most 50 records per delta, slow requests dropped, state JSON compressed once per change. Behind a reverse proxy you control, set `TRUST_PROXY=1` so the limits use the client address from `X-Forwarded-For`.
- Live updates through server-sent events (`/api/events`); `GET`/`POST /api/galaxy` and `/api/chat` read the state and send deltas, in the contracts' JSON shapes.

```bash
npm install && npm run build:web     # -> dist/
npm start                            # http://localhost:8080 (env: PORT, DB=distant-worlds.sqlite, DIST=dist, APP_PATH=/, ADMIN=<hex pubkey>)

# dev: the server in one terminal, vite (proxies /api) in another
npm start & npm run dev:web          # http://localhost:5173
```

With Docker (the database lives in the `data` volume; CapRover uses the same `Dockerfile` through `captain-definition`: give the app a persistent directory mounted at `/data` and container port 8080):

```bash
docker compose up -d --build         # http://localhost:8080 (PORT and APP_PATH from the environment or .env)
```

Known limits: the server trusts no one more than the contracts did (same self-declared homes and honest-rate miner), and the database has to be backed up like any file. Behind a sub-path set `APP_PATH` (e.g. `/dw/`, with the trailing slash): it is part of every explorer's delegation, so changing it invalidates every key signed in so far (explorers have to sign in again).
